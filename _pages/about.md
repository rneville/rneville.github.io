I'm an applied mathematician specializing in topological data analysis. My research develops methods for finding structure, order, and regularity in complex, multi-scale data, from simulated spatiotemporal systems to real-world experimental data. I work closely with interdisciplinary teams to turn open-ended questions into rigorous computational analyses, and I'm passionate about explaining technical ideas clearly to any audience. I work primarily in Python (NumPy, scikit-learn, PyTorch) and helped develop open-source tools such as [Persistence Images](/projects/2_persistence_images/), a feature-engineering method cited 1,400+ times.

Most recently, I was an Assistant Professor of Mathematics at Northern Arizona University. Before that, I was a postdoc at the University of Arizona and earned my PhD in Mathematics at Colorado State University. I've taught over 40 university-level courses across the curriculum, from calculus to applied math for chemists to algebraic topology. Over the last eight years, I overhauled my teaching to better support student learning, implementing inquiry-based learning, flipped classrooms, and mastery-based grading.

I'm currently looking for roles as a **data scientist** or **machine learning engineer**. Take a look at some of my [projects](/projects/), or get in touch using the links below.
