Over fifteen years at Colorado State University, the University of Arizona, and Northern Arizona University, I've taught more than 40 university courses across the mathematics curriculum. Teaching is where I built many of the skills I bring to data science:

- **Communicating technical ideas** to audiences with very different backgrounds, from first-year biology majors to graduate students.
- **Designing assessments that measure what matters.** Mastery-based grading meant defining clear learning objectives and building evaluations around them.
- **Iterating on evidence.** Over the last eight years I redesigned my courses around inquiry-based learning and flipped classrooms, adjusting based on what student work showed was and wasn't working.
- **Mentoring and running programs** for students and instructors.

- **Differential Equations**

### Featured courses

**Applied Mathematics for Chemists I & II** (Colorado State University). Developed in close collaboration with chemistry faculty, this two-semester sequence teaches a variety of mathematical topics tailored specifically to chemistry applications, in particular physical chemistry. Building it meant working with chemistry faculty to identify the mathematics their students actually needed, then designing the course around those needs. We described the course in the _Journal of Chemical Education_: [Applied Mathematics for Chemistry Majors](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jchemed.8b00107) (2018).
